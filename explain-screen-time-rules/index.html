FAMILY ROUTINES

How to explain screen-time rules to children

Screen-time limits are usually easier to follow when they are visible, predictable and discussed before they are needed. A simple weekly plan can help the whole family use the same language.

Start with the routine, not the restriction

Rather than opening with “you can’t have your tablet,” explain what happens next: breakfast, school, play, dinner, tablet time, bedtime. Device time is then one part of the day, not the whole argument.

Use clear, concrete words

Children often understand “tablet time starts at 3:00 pm” better than “you have two hours left today.” Try using the same short labels each time:

  • Tablet time: when the device can be used.
  • Tablet off: when it is time for another part of the day.
  • Daily limit: the maximum, not a target to use every day.

Make the plan visible

A timetable on the fridge, kitchen wall or family noticeboard removes the need for anyone to remember every rule. Look at it together when you first make it, then refer back to it calmly when the day gets busy.

Keep it consistent, but talk about changes

Weekends, holidays and special occasions may need different arrangements. If the schedule changes, tell your child before the usual tablet-time moment and update the visible plan when you can.

Questions parents often ask

Should children help make the screen-time plan?

Where it suits your family, involving them in the conversation can help them understand the plan. Parents still decide the rules and can adapt them as needed.

What should I do when the rules change?

Say what is changing and when it applies, then update the timetable. It is easier to handle a change before device time begins than in the middle of it.
